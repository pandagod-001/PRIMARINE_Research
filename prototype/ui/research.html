Validated Research Evidence

Research Question

Can calibrated freight-rate uncertainty provide a useful signal for false-breakout exposure and support selective abstention in procurement timing?

Method

Single-step point forecasts are augmented with distribution-free Split Conformalized Quantile Regression (Split-CQR) calibrated at nominal 90% coverage. Prediction interval width $W_t = U_t - L_t$ is compared against a validation-derived decision threshold ($\tau = 1.35 \times \text{median width} = 5.9529$).

Key Finding

Higher conformal interval width was observationally associated with greater observed false-breakout exposure in the evaluated scenarios:

Regime Observed False Breakout Rate Discrimination Metric
High-Width Regime ($W > \tau$) 40.25% $\text{AUC} = \mathbf{0.6719}$
($p < 0.01$)
Low-Width Regime ($W \le \tau$) 23.39%

Selective Abstention

Gating decisions using the pre-calibrated threshold on held-out scenarios reduced observed false breakouts while retaining substantial decision coverage:

  • 86 → 41 observed false-breakout events (52.33% reduction)
  • 61.11% decision coverage
  • +$0.0334/MT (+0.453%) nominal cost difference (Wilcoxon $p = 5.42 \times 10^{-11}$)

Negative Controls

  • Spot Boundary Crossing: $\text{AUC} = 0.5025$ (99.65% interval crossing rate; degenerate signal).
  • Directional Sign Error: $\text{AUC} = 0.4487$ (width measures spread, not binary sign correctness).

Limitations

This result is based on the evaluated research population (Baltic Dry Index 2018–2024 testbed) and does not establish universal real-world performance. The prototype does not execute live trades or connect to live satellite AIS.
