PRIMARINE — Uncertainty-Gated Procurement Research Prototype

Conformal Prediction Interval Width as a Decision-Support Signal for Maritime Freight Procurement

Empirical Data: BDI Testbed (N=288) Pre-Calibrated τ = 5.9529 Wilcoxon p = 5.42e-11

Core Research Question

"Can calibrated freight-rate forecast uncertainty provide useful information about false-breakout exposure and support selective abstention in maritime freight procurement decisions?"

In the evaluated scenarios on the Baltic Dry Index benchmark (2018–2024), calibrated Split-CQR prediction interval width was observationally associated with false-breakout risk under hard physical constraints.

Negative Results & Research Boundaries

Spot Boundary-Crossing Degeneracy

AUC = 0.5025 (99.65% interval crossing rate)

Boundary crossing did not provide useful discrimination in the evaluated experiment. Daily volatility spreads encompass normal price deltas unconditionally.

Directional Sign Error Non-Correlation

AUC = 0.4487

CQR width did not show useful discrimination of binary single-step directional error. Width indicates macro volatility spread, not point forecast sign correctness.

What the Evaluated Research Demonstrates

The evaluated experiments provide empirical evidence that calibrated conformal interval width was associated with false-breakout exposure and that a pre-calibrated abstention policy reduced observed false-breakout events by 52.33% while retaining partial decision coverage under rigid physical constraints.

This prototype demonstrates that research mechanism using research-derived data and controlled decision logic. It is not evidence of production or real-world deployment.

What this Prototype Does NOT Do

  • Connect to live freight feeds or real-time APIs
  • Connect to satellite AIS or track real vessels
  • Query live port authority berth queues
  • Execute binding charter party contracts autonomously
  • Guarantee conformal coverage under prospective structural breaks
  • Replace authorized human procurement decision-makers
